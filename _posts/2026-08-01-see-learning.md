SEE learning (Social, Emotional, and Ethical Learning) is a K-12 education program developed by Emory University in collaboration with His Holiness the Dalai Lama. With the intent to educate both the heart and the mind, the SEE Learning program provides a practical, science-backed approach to nurturing the whole child without any religious or methodical orientation. It equips educators with adaptable, easy-to-use lessons and ongoing professional development, focusing on essential life skills like focused attention, self-compassion, emotional resilience, trauma-related awareness, systems thinking, and ethical decision-making. These skills are structured across three interconnected areas—understanding oneself, understanding others, and understanding the broader community—through the lenses of awareness, compassion, and active engagement.

At Sahyadri School KFI, this approach fits naturally into everyday school life, where quiet spaces during morning assemblies, silent meals, and Astachal are already deeply valued. Through simple practices of sensorial and spatial attention training as well as self-observation, this approach helps students build a functional "understanding of the mind"—teaching them to observe how thoughts and feelings arise, recognize emotional triggers before reacting, and navigate complex inner states with clarity. 

The SEE framework introduces practices, such as grounding and nervous system self-regulation, ensuring that students do not just talk about emotions but physically learn how to soothe their bodies when feeling overwhelmed. Beyond individual well-being, the curriculum emphasizes compassionate systems thinking, helping students realize their deep interdependence with others, cultivate ethical discernment, and recognize how their personal choices impact their surrounding environment. 

For students, integrating these reflective habits with structured emotional training offers profound benefits: it helps them recognize and manage their stress, build healthier relationships with peers through deep empathy, and stay grounded during difficult moments. By learning to tune into their internal feelings, students develop a strong sense of self-awareness, emotional self-regulation, and long-term mental resilience. Ultimately, this balanced foundation enables them to think critically, navigate complex social situations with kindness, and approach the world around them as thoughtful, responsible individuals.
